BOMSC-Net: Boundary Optimization and
Multi-Scale Context Awareness Based Building
Extraction From High-Resolution
Remote Sensing Imagery
Yuan Zhou , Zhanlong Chen ,B i nW a n g , Shuangjiang Li, Hao Liu, Daozhu Xu, and Chao Ma
Abstract— Automatic building extraction from high-resolution
remote sensing imagery has various applications, such as urban
planning and land use management. However, the existing
methods for automatic building extraction focus on regional
accuracy but not on boundary quality. As a result, these building
extraction methods yield discontinuous results due to tree and
shadow occlusions and complex building roof materials. In this
article, BOMSC-Net, which can consider boundary optimization
and multi-scale context awareness, is proposed to address these
two problems. BOMSC-Net is an encoder-to-decoder network
composed of a basic network, a multi-scale context-aware module
(MSCAM), and a direction feature optimization module (DFOM).
The basic network learns to predict building segmentation maps
through attention gates (AGs) that connect low-level feature
jumps to advanced semantics. MSCAM is used as a bridge, which
uses graph reasoning blocks (GRBs) to solve intra-class discon-
tinuities. Finally, the DFOM constructs a direction ﬁeld (DF)
and then uses direction information for boundary correction to
achieve ﬁne building segmentations. Experiments were conducted
on the open Massachusetts building dataset, WHU aerial building
dataset, and Inria aerial image labeling dataset. The quantitative
and qualitative analyses demonstrate that the proposed BOMSC-
Net can help building extraction overcome interruptions caused
by shadows and occlusions, extract buildings with different scales
and materials, and realize boundary optimization and complete
segmentation of buildings.
Index Terms — Boundary optimization, building extraction,
direction ﬁeld (DF), graph reasoning block (GRB), multi-scale
context-aware module (MSCAM).

I. I NTRODUCTION
A
S CENTRAL locations of human activities, cities are
of great signiﬁcance, so realizing effective analysis and
evaluation of a city’s development process is signiﬁcant for
guiding sustainable development of the region [1]. As one of
the main constituent elements of a city, buildings are inevitably
a focus of researchers. Remote sensing imagery is currently the
mainstream source for obtaining ground feature information.
Therefore, effective extraction of buildings from remote sens-
ing imagery is vital to applications such as urban planning,
land use management, resource allocation, and convenience
services [2]. Although high-resolution remote sensing imagery
is rich in feature information, co mplex spectrum, structures,
and texture information of feat ures also present severe chal-
lenges to accurately extracting buildings.
In the past, the use of image-processing-based methods
enabled the possibility of building extraction. These research
methods can be divided into methods based on building
features [3], [4] and methods based on auxiliary informa-
tion [5]. The methods based on building features use spectrum,
texture, geometry, and other building characteristics to extract
the building. Among them, corner detection operators [6],
edge detection operators [7], image transformation [8], and
histograms [9] are widely used in building feature extraction.
However, because trees and shadows often block buildings,
various detection operators ca nnot fully use the general geo-
metric information. Some researchers construct a template
library based on the prior shape of the building and introduce
it into the active contour model to constrain the evolution
of the segmentation curve [10], [11]. However, this method
is limited regarding complex and diverse building shapes in
a large area. The methods based on auxiliary information
combine multi-source remote sensing data [12], [13], GIS
data [14], and other rich building feature information to
achieve accurate extraction. However, these methods are often
time-consuming and extremely costly. This method of relying
on expert experience and manually designed features is only
suitable for some speciﬁc tasks. It is currently difﬁcult to meet
research needs through this approach [15], [16].
In recent years, owing to the advantages of being able to
learn discriminatory features actively, deep learning has been

widely used in the ﬁeld of remote sensing, including change
detection [17], [18], scene recognition [19], [20], and land
use classiﬁcation [21], [22]. Among them, the most typical
method is the convolutional neural network (CNN) [23].
CNN can obtain hierarchical data representation. High-level
feature representation relies on low-level feature represen-
tation. With the construction of a multi-layer convolution
operation from shallow to deep, the features with more
advanced semantic information are extracted gradually. Then,
the fully convolutional network (FCN) [24] was proposed,
which successfully broke the patch-wise barrier by removing
the fully connected layer and achieved pixel-wise perfor-
mance. This is essential for this data-intensive task of building
extraction.
Inspired by the above, the method based on deep learning
offers excellent performance in building extraction [25], [26].
Furthermore, some studies improved the FCN framework to
obtain high-precision building segmentation results [27]–[29].
However, accurate extraction o f buildings from remote sens-
ing images is often affected by many objective conditions.
On one hand, due to the spatial diversity of the building
itself, its shape is complex, the size differs, and the roof
material may be different; on the other hand, the imaging
conditions of remote sensing images are disturbed by the
environment, and buildings are often blocked by shadows and
trees. These effects make it difﬁcult for the divided buildings
to maintain their unique morphological characteristics and
integrity.
The boundary can describe the morphological shape of the
building well [30]. Therefore, some studies achieve ﬁne build-
ing segmentation by enhancing the description of boundary
information or using postprocessing techniques. For example,
Jung et al. [31] proposed a method composed of holistically
nested edge detection and a boundary enhancement module,
which extracts edge features and a ssists in generating building
segmentation results with enhanced boundary information.
Ma et al. [32] constructed an encoder with local and global
feature extraction to fully describe buildings of different
shapes. Although this method can obtain useful information
that pays more attention to the boundary, adding a complex
network structure and additional auxiliary data also increases
consumption. Postprocessing technology is a commonly used
method to solve blurred boundaries of building segmentation.
Some ﬁlters, such as a guided ﬁlter [33], morphological
ﬁlter [34], and other methods, optimize building boundaries
and improve boundary regularity. In addition, some methods
try to reﬁne the results of FCNs using probabilistic graph
models, such as fully connected conditional random ﬁelds
(CRFs) [35]. For example, Zhu et al. [36] proposed to com-
bine segmentation-prior CRFs to carry out building extraction
work based on a multi-scale semantic segmentation network
(D-LinkNet). Sun et al. [37] proposed a new time-efﬁcient
end-to-end CRF model to reduce time consumption. Although
the postprocessing method has been greatly improved, its
computational cost is high, and the time consumption problem
cannot be completely solved. To further improve building
shape, a multi-task framework is applied to building extraction.
Wu et al. [38] proposed a boundary regulated network, which
uses an improved U-Net to perform multi-task learning to
obtain segmentation maps and edge contour maps, to obtain
the results of boundary optimization. However, there is no
direct connection between tasks, and the burden of model
training will continually increase.
The result of building extr action is also reﬂected in com-
pleteness. Some studies have attempted to capture the con-
textual information of multi-scale objects by constructing a
wider [39], [40] and deeper [41] network. Most of these
methods use pyramid structures or convolution operations with
different receptive ﬁelds to fuse features of different scales
and resolutions to enhance contextual information learning
of buildings. Sun et al. [42] proposed using an improved
encoder with multi-scale convolution to extract depth features
of buildings. Some researchers used spatial pyramid pooling
operations to connect the encoder and decoder features hierar-
chically to extract multi-scale building information [43], [44].
Although feature fusion of different resolutions in the process
of feature extraction can enhance the contextual information
of the building, the location information of the building
may be lost, leading to blurry boundaries or excessively
smooth corners. Introducing skip connections [45] in the
decoder and capturing long-distance dependencies in space
or channel through the attention mechanism [46], [47] rep-
resent important ways to solve the lack of spatial infor-
mation. Yang et al. [48] combined a dense network and
attention mechanism to form a new dense attention network.
The purpose is to enhance low-level feature information
and prevent excessively redundant low-level features from
being introduced into the model. Pan et al. [49] combined
space and channel attention mechanisms to construct a new
generative adversarial network to achieve ﬁne segmentation
of buildings. The incomplete segmentation caused by the
difﬁculty of capturing long-distance area has not yet been
solved.
However, unclear boundaries or variable intensity lead to
the lack of feature correlation between pixels. The global
correlation between long-distance regions cannot be guar-
anteed. There are still problems of blurred boundaries and
incomplete extraction in building extraction. To solve the
above problems, BOMSC-Net is proposed to achieve ﬁne
segmentation of multi-scale buildings. On one hand, with the
introduction of attention gates (AGs) in the basic network, low-
level features are connected to ad vanced semantic information
to realize attention to signiﬁcant areas and suppress irrele-
vant background. In addition, a direction feature optimization
module (DFOM) is used to further perceive the direction infor-
mation of buildings to reﬁne the results of building boundary
segmentation. On the other hand, a multi-scale context-aware
module (MSCAM) is introduced. Instead of stacking deep
convolution operations to implicitly construct the global con-
text, the module complements the global information of build-
ings through graph reasoning blocks (GRBs). Experiments
were conducted on the open Massachusetts building dataset,
WHU aerial building dataset, and Inria aerial image labeling
dataset. Quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrate that
BOMSC-Net achieves outstanding performance in terms of
generalization and robustness.

II. M ETHODOLOGY
This study proposed a multi-module CNN network frame-
work, BOMSC-Net, to extract buildings from remote sens-
ing images. The network comprises the basic network, the
MSCAM, and the DFOM. As shown in Fig. 1, the basic
network uses multi-layer convolution operations on the input
RGB images to process local information layer by layer to
obtain higher dimensional semantic information.
In addition, the basic network connects low-level features to
high-level semantic information through AGs to direct atten-
tion to signiﬁcant areas of buildings and to suppress irrelevant
backgrounds. In particular, an MSCAM is embedded between
encoding and decoding to realize the inference of global
context information to better express multi-scale features of
buildings. To further reﬁne the ﬁnal segmentation results,
this study designs a DFOM to dig deeper into the spatial
relationships between each pixe l and uses the directional
spatial relationship as a guide to achieve reﬁned segmentation
of the network results.

B. Multi-Scale Context-Aware Module
To enhance the network’s ability to reason regarding contex-
tual information, thereby cap turing feature maps with global
contextual information, this study designs an MSCAM to
aggregate semantic informa tion between remote regions of
different scales. In Fig. 4, pat hs with different pooling sizes
are constructed hierarchically into a feature map. GRBs are
applied to each layer of paths to achieve efﬁcient global
information inference.
Fig. 4. Illustration of MSCAM. In particular, the ﬁgure shows the structure
of the GRB in detail.
Fig. 5. Schematic of the inference space.
The MSCAM is divided into four paths. First, we perform
a3 × 3 convolution operation on the input feature map
X. Then, three paths must undergo max-pooling operations
with different kernel sizes. Speciﬁcally, the kernel sizes for
max-pooling operations are set to 2 × 2, 3 × 3, and 5 ×
5. To preserve the original features, the remaining path is
not pooled. We use different receptive ﬁelds to capture and
learn multi-scale building inf ormation. Second, these features
are fed into the GRBs to better learn the object’s remote
semantic context. Finally, the feature maps of each branch path
after passing through the GRB are uniﬁed through bilinear
interpolation, and then the ﬁnal output feature map X′ is
obtained through concatenation.
Graph reasoning has been proven to be an effective method
of relationship reasoning for obtaining object relationships
between remote regions [50], [51]. To obtain global context
information for buildings, this study projects a feature map
into an inference space that can in teractively describe regional
relationships to capture the distance between disjoint regions
with arbitrarily shaped relationships.
Different from the regular coordinate space ℧ =
{1,..., H ×{1,..., W, the latent inference space expresses
the relationship between regions through nodes, edges, and
adjacency matrices. Concretely , the convolution is performed
over a regular nearest neighbor graph deﬁned by an adjacent
matrix A ∈ RL×L ,w h e r eAij = 1 if regions i and j are
spatially adjacent; otherwise, Aij = 0. The edges of the graph
encode spatial proximity, and its node stores the feature for
that location, as shown in Fig. 5.

V. C ONCLUSION
This study explores the shortcomings of the existing meth-
ods for extracting buildings from high-resolution remote sens-
ing images based on deep learning. Inaccurate boundary
extraction and incomplete building extraction are the key
problems of current building extraction. Therefore, this study
proposes a building extraction method (BOMSC-Net) that
simultaneously considers boundary optimization and multi-
scale context awareness to address inaccurate building bound-
aries and incomplete extraction results. The experimental
results on three open-source datasets show that the pro-
posed BOMSC-Net can optimize boundaries and enhance
context awareness. In complex environments, such as those
with shadow and tree occlusions, BOMSC-Net can accurately
extract the boundaries of buildings and can achieve com-
plete extraction of buildings of different scales. To verify
the performance of our network, this study not only uses
general semantic segmentation evaluation metrics but also
uses boundary evaluation metrics to quantitatively illustrate
the advantages of BOMSC-Net in boundary optimization.
Compared with previous work, the extraction results are
signiﬁcantly improved. The test results include higher R-
value, F1-score, and IoU than the comparison method, indi-
cating that our method can maintain a balance between R-
value and P-value and reduce false an d missed segmentation.
BOMSC-Net achieves excellent scores for 95% HD and SSIM,
showing that it can achieve building boundary optimiza-
tion. This study also includes ablation experiments to verify
that MSCAM and DFOM signiﬁcantly improve the network
performance.
However, the method in this study has some shortcomings.
BOMSC-Net may have the same enhancement effect on fea-
tures similar to buildings, so there would be some misclas-
siﬁcations in places where build ings and similar features are
present. The method of GRB would be restricted in cases of
severe occlusion by shadows and trees. We will consider multi-
spectral and multi-source data fusion to solve these problems
in future work.
