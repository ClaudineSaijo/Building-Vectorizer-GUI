Abstract— This article presents two amodal instance seg-
mentation (AIS) datasets in the field of remote sensing: the
multiview building dataset for the Zurich region (MVB-Zurich)
and the multisize building dataset for the Dortmund region
(MSB-Dortmund). Additionally, a new AIS network framework,
named FE-RSBL-AmodalNet, is proposed, which is based on
feature enhancement and remote sensing boundary loss. Instance
segmentation has emerged as a popular approach for building
extraction in recent years. However, a limitation of using such
algorithms for building extraction is the inability to predict the
invisible areas. Consequently, the extracted building contours
are incomplete, which hampers certain applications relying on
accurate building extraction. To address this limitation, AIS
has emerged as a promising research field. Unfortunately, there
is currently a lack of datasets available for developing AIS
algorithms in the field of remote sensing, which poses a barrier
to the widespread application of AIS in this domain. This article
introduces two AIS datasets specifically designed for remote-
sensing buildings. The datasets consist of images captured from
tilted views using a tilt photography system, resulting in a
significant presence of occluded areas within the images. More-
over, the MVB-Zurich dataset comprises aerial images captured
from five different viewpoints, while the MSB-Dortmund dataset
encompasses diverse buildings, including garages and residences,
with varying sizes. The multiview and multisize attributes of these
datasets offer enhanced research opportunities. Furthermore,
a new AIS framework, specifically designed for remote sensing
buildings, was proposed with the aim of accurately predicting
complete building contours.
Index Terms— Amodal instance segmentation (AIS), building
extraction, dataset, multi size, multi view, occlusion.

The field of building extraction has witnessed continuous
advancements due to the rapid development of deep learning.
Object detection, semantic segmentation, and instance segmen-
tation methods [7] are increasingly employed for extracting
buildings from images. Instance segmentation enables the
distinction of individual buildings and achieves pixel-level
segmentation. However, conventional instance segmentation
methods solely concentrate on the visible regions of the
target, leading to incomplete predicted building contours when
the target is partially occluded. In 2016, Li and Malik [8]
introduced the concept of amodal instance segmentation (AIS),
which extends the segmentation to the invisible regions of
the target, thereby predicting complete object contours. The
term amodal is in contrast to modal, where modal percep-
tual representation refers to sensory qualities (e.g., color or
texture) that are visibly present. Modal percepts represent the
visible regions of a scene, while amodal percepts represent the
invisible regions [9]. The task of AIS holds great significance
for remote sensing image processing applications. AIS-based
building extraction enables the accurate prediction of complete
contours for occluded buildings, addressing a limitation of
existing algorithms.
However, due to the novelty and complexity of the task,
there is currently no large-scale AIS dataset available in the
field of remote sensing. In this article, two AIS datasets,
MVB-Zurich and MSB-Dortmund, were proposed, which were
composed of airborne tilt images. The airborne images are
captured by the inclined photography system, resulting in a
significant number of occlusion areas. To the best of our
knowledge, these are the first two AIS datasets in the field
of remote sensing.
Furthermore, the robustness of the view and size is crucial
for the AIS algorithm. In numerous practical applications, the
size and position of the target object may constantly vary, and
such variations can affect the algorithm’s performance. If the
instance segmentation algorithm is only capable of extracting
targets of a single size, it may fail to fully segment the
target when its size changes and it cannot maintain pixel-level
accuracy simultaneously. When the view is transformed, the
difficulty of extracting the monomerization of buildings will
be different. Therefore, multiview images of buildings within

II. R ELATED WORK
In this section, the shortcomings of existing datasets and
algorithms are analyzed to illustrate the significance of the
proposed datasets and algorithm framework. First, existing
AIS datasets were introduced and their characteristics were
analyzed. Second, the development of building extraction
methods, mainly the deep learning algorithms were introduced.
Finally, the problems of existing datasets and algorithms were
analyzed, and our contributions were proposed.
A. Existing AIS Datasets
Currently, the majority of existing AIS datasets are based
on natural images. The first natural image AIS dataset was
published by Li and Malik [8], which was based on Pascal
VOC [11]. In addition, the conventional AIS datasets include
COCOA [13] based on MS-Coco [10], KINS dataset [12],
and D2S amodal dataset [14] annotated based on D2S [15].
However, natural images exhibit a scarcity of building targets
and lack multiview and multisize information. Hence, natural
images are unsuitable for researching AIS algorithms intended
for building extraction in remote sensing.
Unfortunately, in the field of remote sensing, there is cur-
rently no dataset specifically designed for AIS research. Some
existing datasets used for building extraction in remote sensing
were annotated in a semantic way [16]. Fig. 1 illustrates the
dataset released by the Inria competition [17]. This dataset
consists of two categories: buildings and non-buildings. With

(b) ground-truth.
the advancements in deep learning, instance segmentation
enabled more precise extraction of buildings in remote sensing
images. The Semcity Toulouse dataset [18] is shown in Fig. 2.
Unlike Inria datasets, this dataset distinguishes individual
instances with different colors, which means instance-level
annotations. The building dataset with instance-level annota-
tions also includes SpaceNet [19], crowdAI Mapping Chal-
lenge [20], and iSAID dataset [21]. In addition, the 2023 IEEE
GRSS data fusion competition [22] unveiled a large-scale
urban building dataset, which encompassed 12 distinct types
of building roofs and can be utilized for instance segmentation.
However, it only includes annotations for the building roofs,
and instances with occlusion are infrequent, making it less
suitable for AIS research.
While there are numerous large building datasets available
in the field of remote sensing, these datasets do have certain
drawbacks, including the following:
1) These datasets primarily capture the top surface infor-
mation of buildings, neglecting occluded targets.
2) The current datasets lack the essential characteristics of
multiview and multisize, which are vital for studying
AIS algorithms related to remote sensing image analysis.
Therefore, the existing datasets in the field of remote sensing
cannot be utilized for studying AIS algorithms focused on
building extraction, and they are insufficient in providing
multiview and multisize research capabilities.
B. Building Extraction Method
Traditional building extraction methods can be categorized
into two types: individual segmentation-oriented extraction
methods and feature-based extraction methods [39], [40].
With the application of deep learning methods in various
fields, object detection, semantic segmentation, and instance
segmentation can provide new solutions for building extraction
in oblique remote sensing images. Different from other meth-
ods, instance segmentation realizes the semantic classification
at the pixel level while locating multiple target buildings.
So far, there have been some frameworks for instance seg-
mentation, such as bottom-up semantic segmentation-based
method and top-down object detection-based method [29].
The bottom-up approach, based on semantic segmentation,
initially segments pixels at the semantic level. Subsequently,
techniques such as pixel clustering are employed to further
segment the image into distinct instances of the same category
[26], [27], [28]. The top-down approach first detects the target

and conducts pixel-level segmentation within the detected
boundary to generate masks for each instance. As instance
segmentation progresses, single-stage algorithms have also
emerged. These include anchor-based methods, represented
by YOLACT [30] and SOLO [31], as well as anchor-free
methods, represented by BlendMask [32] and PolarMask [33].
Although the conventional instance segmentation method
achieves pixel-level segmentation, it primarily focuses on
the visible area of the target. Consequently, it encounters
challenges when attempting to extract the complete contour
of partially occluded targets. To address this limitation, the
concept of AIS emerged. AIS goes beyond traditional instance
segmentation by specifically targeting the invisible areas to
achieve complete segmentation of occluded targets. Li and
Malik [8] generated detection boxes and masks for the visible
area of the object, and further expanded the mask by iterative
bounding box expansion to iteratively generate the boundary
box and mask containing the occluded area of the image. Zhu
et al. [13] directly predicted amodal masks based on Amodal-
Mask. Follmann et al. [14] proposed an end-to-end network
that predicts amodal instance masks, allowing the forward
propagation process to predict both visible and invisible areas
of the target. Zhang et al. [34] introduced semantics-aware
distance map, along with the semantic layering network, which
are designed for amodal segmentation. Additionally, Ke et al.
[38] proposed BCNet, a bi-layer convolutional network that
effectively separates the occludee and occluded objects in
highly overlapped scenarios.
The existing AIS algorithms based on natural image
research proved to be inadequate when applied to building
datasets with complex backgrounds and diverse target shapes,
failing to effectively address the challenge of extracting com-
plete contours for occluded targets.
Based on the shortcomings of the existing datasets and
the inadaptability of the current AIS algorithm and remote
sensing data, two AIS datasets and an AIS framework were
proposed.
The datasets address the absence of AIS datasets in the
current remote sensing field, as well as the lack of multiview
and multisize AIS datasets. The datasets specifically annotate
the building occlusion category, facilitating the use of conven-
tional instance segmentation algorithms to analyze occluded
characteristics and offering new insights for AIS research. In
summary, our contributions are as follows:
1) MVB-Zurich dataset was proposed. The dataset con-
tained a large number of occlusion areas, accounting
for 9.04% of the total building area, which can be
used for the study of AIS algorithms in the field
of remote sensing. Further, the MVB-Zurich dataset
includes images captured from the same area from five
different views, showcasing its multiview characteristics,
which we analyze in this article.
2) The MSB-Dortmund dataset obtained from the oblique
view was proposed. The dataset contained a large num-
ber of occlusion areas, accounting for 4.7% of the total
building area. This dataset is suitable for studying AIS
algorithms in remote sensing and covers a wide range
of building sizes, from small targets like garages to
large targets like residential buildings. In this article,
we analyze its multisize characteristics.
3) In order to improve the inadaptability of the current
AIS algorithms and remote sensing datasets, a new
AIS framework, FE-RSBL-AmodalNet, was proposed.
According to the characteristics of remote sensing
images, an FE module based on the self-attention mech-
anism and RSBL was added to the framework.
In Section III, the characteristics and annotation rules of
the two datasets were introduced in detail. In Section IV, the
proposed framework was introduced. In Section V, experi-
ments were carried out based on the proposed datasets and
frameworks. Finally, the article was summarized.
III. D ATASETS : MVB-Z URICH AND MSB-D ORTMUND
This section provides a detailed introduction to the char-
acteristics and annotation rules of the two datasets. It covers
the dataset’s source, the number of images and instances, and
the annotation process. Furthermore, it includes an analysis
of the multiview buildings in MVB-Zurich and the multisize
buildings in MSB-Dortmund.
A. Data Collection
The images in the MVB-Zurich dataset were captured in
the Zurich region, utilizing data acquired from the benchmark
project “High Density Aerial Image Matching Benchmark”
[23]. The data were acquired using the medium format camera
Leica RCD30 Oblique Penta, which has a pixel size of 6 µm
and a radiation resolution of 14 bits. The tilt angle of the
four cameras mounted on corners was 35 ◦, and the GSD of
the resulting image was 6–13 cm. The nadir image had a
GSD of 6 cm. The aircraft, equipped with a tilt photography
system, captures the Zurich area from five different viewpoints
(referred to as view1, view2, view3, view4, and view5). The
data images from each viewpoint exhibited varying degrees of
building occlusions.
The acquired images primarily featured large single-family
buildings, with certain areas containing multiple adjacent
buildings. Fig. 3 depicts a schematic of the annotated area
in Zurich. Fig. 4 illustrates the shapes of both single buildings
and adjacent buildings.
The MSB-Dortmund dataset was derived from oblique air-
borne data acquired by ISPRS as part of the ISPRS Science
Program’s Multi-Platform Ultrahigh Resolution Photogramme-
try benchmarking project [24]. The ground sampling distance
(GSD) of these images was 10 cm in the nadir view and
ranged from 8 to 12 cm in the oblique view. Fig. 5 illus-
trates a schematic of the annotated area in Dortmund. The
dataset of this project includes single buildings and adjacent
buildings, as depicted in Fig. 6. Single buildings and adjacent
buildings exhibit significant color variations in their roofs and
facades. Notably, the dataset includes buildings with signif-
icant variations in size, including scenes where buildings of
different sizes are adjacent or irregular buildings are adjacent.
Section III-C of this article discusses the size characteristics
of the dataset.

B. Data Annotation
The image annotation tool Labelme [25] was used to anno-
tate the datasets. At present, some mainstream deep learning
algorithms have requirements on the size of the input image. If
Fig. 6. Building example in MSB-Dortmund: (a) single building and
(b) adjacent building.
the pixel size of the input image is excessively large, it not only
places high demands on computer hardware but also hinders
the neural network’s ability to effectively learn building infor-
mation. This article adopts the approach depicted in Fig. 7.
Initially, the original remote sensing image is annotated using
the graphical annotation tool, resulting in a json annotation
file in Labelme format. Subsequently, it is transformed into a
semantic map to acquire the semantic labels. Then, the original
image and its corresponding semantic label are cropped with
a fixed step size. Every small image clipped from the original
image is associated with its respective semantic label, and
subsequently, the semantic label is converted into an instance
label that corresponds to the small image. If images of different
sizes and their corresponding annotation information were
needed, it only needs to take different steps to complete the
synchronous cropping of the original image and its semantic
labels, avoiding secondary labeling.
The annotation software would display different labels in
distinct colors. Fig. 8 illustrates the schematic of the annotation
process, where the yellow polygon represents the occlusion
area, the green polygon represents the overall building area,
and the red polygon represents the roof of the building. In
cases where a building was partially occluded, the labeling
process involved predicting the true boundaries of the building
first, followed by annotating the complete outlines of the
building.
For the MVB-Zurich dataset, a total of 2304 small images
with a size of 640 × 640 pixels were obtained. In this dataset,
two categories are annotated, the overall building category
“building” and the occluded building category “occlusion.”
For the MSB-Dortmund dataset, a total of 653 small images
with a size of 500 × 500 pixels were obtained. The dataset
includes three annotated categories: “building,” “roof,” and
“occlusion,” which respectively represent the entire building,
the building roof, and the area of the building that is occluded.
Dense annotations were provided for these images. Specific
annotation rules are introduced below. The annotation file
contained the image’s path, name, size, and vertex informa-
tion of the annotated polygons. Due to the complexity of
occlusion, only two types were taken into account. One was

IV. FE-RSBL-A MODEL NET
In this section, FE-RSBL-AmodelNet, as shown in Fig. 15,
is proposed for the characteristics of oblique remote sensing
images and the difficulties of building segmentation. This
framework includes a feature extraction structure. Then the
obtained feature enters the FPN and FCOS structures [45].
Finally, the result is obtained by the mask segmentation
module.
First, to address the issue of complex background, a FE
module based on the self-attention mechanism [46] is intro-
duced. This module allows the network to mitigate the interfer-
ence from background information and focus on the essential
features of the target. Furthermore, to address the challenges
posed by occlusion and the difficulty in distinguishing adjacent
buildings caused by oblique multiview, the RSBL function
[42] is employed. This loss function is used to constrain
the predictions of occluded and non-occluded targets by the
bi-layer segmentation part, thereby enhancing the network’s
Fig. 15. FE-RSBL-AmodelNet framework.
attention to occluded and boundary regions, and ultimately
enabling the differentiation between occluded buildings and
adjacent buildings.
A. FE Module Based on Self-Attention
The self-attention mechanism has its origins in text feature
extraction. In deep learning tasks, different feature vectors
contribute to the task to varying extents. Certain features
are crucial for target localization and segmentation, whereas
others, such as background feature vectors, have a lesser
impact on task completion. Thus, it is essential to amplify
the impact of effective features in the network while reducing
the influence of features with weak correlations. This entails
assigning different weights to features to regulate their contri-
bution during the model learning process.
The self-attention mechanism enables the network to adap-
tively learn the correlation degree between the features on
the feature map in the global scope, so as to calculate the
influence degree between the components on the feature map,
determine its weight, and extract the important information
from the redundant information more effectively [41]. This is
particularly crucial for remote sensing images with complex
background information, as it enables more accurate removal

B. RSBL
1) Bi-Layer Segmentation Branch: The challenge in pre-
dicting occluded targets lies in the fact that the occluded
area is concealed, making it difficult to learn the features
within. Hence, in the mask segmentation stage, this section
employs a bi-layer structure based on RSBL to hierarchically
predict the occluded and occluding objects. The prediction
outcome of the occlusion is utilized as auxiliary information
for predicting the building target, thus accomplishing the
overall prediction of the building target.
Fig. 20 illustrates that the upper branch employs the anno-
tated occlusion category as the supervision information, while
the lower branch uses the annotated building. The occlusion
features generated by the upper branch serve as auxiliary
inputs to the lower branch, together with the feature map
obtained in the previous step through the residual method,
for predicting the overall building area. In the absence of
occlusion, the occlusion branch does not produce output.
Instead, the feature information acquired in the previous
step directly enters the branch responsible for predicting the
occluded object, ultimately yielding the building mask.
2) Remote Sensing Boundary Loss Function (RSBL): Seg-
mentation of adjacent and densely populated buildings from
multiple views poses a challenge. To address this issue, this
section introduces the RSBL function based on BF1 [43], [44]
in the segmentation part. RSBL aims to overcome the net-
work’s limitations in dealing with such scenarios by enhancing
its focus on the building’s boundary, improving its ability
to represent boundaries, and distinguishing distinct building
instances within adjacent or dense scenes. While loss functions
are typically used to measure the difference between a mask
and the ground truth in instance segmentation, this approach
fails to fully attend to and correct discrepancies between the
predicted boundary and the ground truth boundary. RSBL
addresses this by monitoring the building’s boundary through-
out the training process, measuring the difference between
boundary pixels and ground truth boundary pixels.
To construct the boundary loss, it is necessary to obtain the
target’s boundary in both the predicted value and the ground
truth value. To achieve this, as depicted in (6), the Softmax

2) Comparative Experiments on Self-Attention Structure:
In the comparison between multihead attention and multilayer
attention structures for self-attention, the multihead attention
mechanism involves dividing or replicating the input matrix
along the channel dimension. This division allows the model
to attend to different aspects of information by treating them as
separate subspaces. The self-attention mechanism is then com-
puted on these distinct channel groups or replicated channel
groups. Finally, the outputs of the multihead self-attention are
combined through channel concatenation or feature addition
to obtain the multihead attention output.
The current FE module based on the self-attention mecha-
nism is incorporated into the last layer of the ResNet feature
extraction structure. To assess the effectiveness of a multilayer
FE structure, this study introduces the FE structure after res3,
res4, and res5 of the ResNet. This allows for a comparison
of whether applying FE at different layers yields improved
results.

3) Comparative Experiments on Boundary Loss Function:
In addition to using the F1 loss for defining the RSBL function,
this study also compares the RSBL based on intersection over
union (IOU). The detailed experimental results are presented
in Tables XV and XVI. These results provide a comparison
of the performance of the boundary loss function based on F1
loss and IOU for remote sensing tasks.
In the boundary region, the number of pixels belonging
to the boundary is typically much smaller compared to the
background pixels in the feature map. Judging the boundary
pixels individually may be influenced by the class imbalance
issue. However, if the boundary can be assessed as a whole,
it can mitigate the impact of this category imbalance. This
article compares the IOU and F1 loss methods for addressing
this challenge. While both IOU and F1 loss methods evaluate
the overall overlap between the predicted boundary and the
ground truth boundary, F1 loss considers both precision and
recall rates, providing a more accurate assessment of the
difference between the predicted and ground truth values. On
the other hand, IOU loss solely focuses on the intersection
ratio between the predicted and ground truth areas, lacking
a more comprehensive evaluation of the difference. Based on
the experimental results, it is observed that F1 loss achieves
higher prediction accuracy, indicating its superior performance
compared to IOU loss.
VI. C ONCLUSION
AIS can solve the problem of traditional instance segmen-
tation, that is, when buildings are occluded, the complete
building contour cannot be extracted. In this article, two AIS
datasets in the field of remote sensing, multiview building
dataset for the Zurich region (MVB-Zurich) and multisize
building dataset for the Dortmund region (MSB-Dortmund),
and a new framework AIS network based on FE and RSBL,
FE-RSBL-AmodalNet, were proposed.
The datasets used in this study, MVB-Zurich and MSB-
Dortmund, were specifically chosen for their abundance of
occlusion areas, making them suitable for investigating AIS in
remote sensing. Additionally, the MVB-Zurich dataset offers
rich multiview characteristics, while the MSB-Dortmund
dataset provides diverse multisize characteristics. These char-
acteristics of the datasets enhance their research value and
contribute to a comprehensive study in this field. The article
explores and analyzes the multiview and multisize characteris-
tics of the datasets to gain deeper insights into AIS in remote
sensing.
For the multiview characteristic of the MVB-Zurich dataset
and the multisize characteristic of the MSB-Dortmund
datasets, there were still several problems as follows:
1) The problems of dense scenes, complex building shapes,
and close colors to the surrounding environment, a large
number of occlusions caused by adjacent buildings,
and incomplete large buildings in small-size images
brought some challenges to the MVB-Zurich dataset.
In addition, experiments on views showed that due to
the differences in images in each view, the network
performed differently on images from different views.
2) The problems of adjacent buildings, complex shapes,
and close to the background brought some challenges
to the MSB-D dataset. In addition, experiments on
different sizes showed that it was more difficult to
predict small-sized buildings, such as garages, followed
by medium-sized buildings.
To address the specific challenges of remote sensing images,
the proposed framework incorporates an FE module based on
the self-attention mechanism and an RSBL. This framework
aims to enhance the attention toward the target and suppress
irrelevant background information. Additionally, by incor-
porating the RSBL, the model gains better capability in
predicting the boundaries of occluded parts of buildings.
The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed
framework performs effectively when buildings are occluded
by heterogeneous objects or are located adjacent to other build-
ings in non-dense scenes. This suggests that the framework
successfully addresses the challenges posed by occlusions
and complex backgrounds, enabling accurate AIS in such
scenarios.
However, for the problems of adjacent buildings in dense
scenes, complex shapes or close to the color of the surround-
ing environment, and incomplete buildings, several instance
segmentation algorithms currently used cannot be solved.
